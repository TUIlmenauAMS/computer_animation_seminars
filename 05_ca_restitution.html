ANIMATION CONCEPT — Restitution: the coefficient of elasticity
Restitution e (0–1) controls how much kinetic energy survives a collision. At e=0 the ball stops dead; at e=1 it bounces back to its original height indefinitely. Real materials: rubber ≈ 0.8, wood ≈ 0.3, clay ≈ 0.05. In Rapier restitution lives on the ColliderDesc — it is a surface property, not a mass property.
RAPIER CONCEPT — Combine rules
When two colliders meet, Rapier combines their values. This demo uses CoefficientCombineRule.Max on each ball so its own restitution dominates regardless of the ground's value — making the grid's variation clean and direct to observe.

ANIMATION CONCEPT — Energy decay per bounce
Each bounce multiplies the ball's speed by e. After n bounces, the ball retains eⁿ of its original kinetic energy. The y-position timeline shows the classic decaying oscillation envelope — the same curve that describes a critically damped spring settling to rest.
ANIMATION CONCEPT — Energy colouring
Ball colour maps to current kinetic energy: red/warm = high KE (fast, just bounced), blue/cool = low KE (nearly at rest). This makes an abstract quantity directly visible — watch the low-restitution balls go cold on first impact while the high-restitution ball stays warm for many bounces.
