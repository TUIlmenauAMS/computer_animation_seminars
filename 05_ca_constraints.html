Demo 3: Joints & Chains

A chain of 8 capsule links connected by revolute joints. The top link is pinned to a fixed anchor. Toggle gravity on and off, then click and drag anywhere on the canvas to aim and fire an impulse at the bottom link — watch the constraint force propagate up the chain. The sidebar shows an animated ragdoll skeleton illustrating how this joint pattern scales to a full character.

ANIMATION CONCEPT — Revolute joints and constraint propagation
A revolute joint removes 5 of the 6 degrees of freedom between two bodies — only rotation around one axis remains free. The joint is enforced each physics step by computing and applying constraint impulses: internal forces that keep the anchor points coincident. When you fire an impulse at the bottom link, the constraint forces travel up the chain in a single solver iteration, producing the characteristic whip-crack response visible here.
RAPIER CONCEPT — createImpulseJoint
Rapier's world.createImpulseJoint(jointData, bodyA, bodyB, wakeUp) creates a joint that is solved via impulse-based constraint projection. The wakeUp flag ensures sleeping bodies are activated when the joint is created. Joint anchors are expressed in each body's local space — not world space — so the joint moves with the body automatically. Joint stiffness is controlled by running multiple substeps per frame rather than one.
ANIMATION CONCEPT — From chain to ragdoll
A ragdoll is this exact pattern at scale. Replace 8 links with ~15 rigid bodies (torso, pelvis, head, upper arms, forearms, thighs, shins, feet) and revolute/spherical joints with angular limits. At character death, the engine takes over from the AnimationMixer: bones are set to the current animated pose and velocity, then handed to Rapier. The sidebar diagram shows the full joint topology of such a skeleton.
