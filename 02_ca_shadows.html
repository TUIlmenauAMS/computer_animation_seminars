THREE.JS CONCEPT — Shadow Map Pipeline
Three.js renders the scene twice when shadows are enabled: first from the light's point of view into a depth texture (the shadow map), then from the camera. During the camera pass, each fragment compares its depth from the light to the stored value — if it is behind something, it is in shadow. The five required steps: renderer.shadowMap.enabled, choose shadowMap.type, light.castShadow, mesh.castShadow, mesh.receiveShadow.
ANIMATION CONCEPT — Shadow Acne & Peter Panning
Set Bias to 0 to see shadow acne: a surface self-shadows itself because of floating-point precision in the depth comparison. Increase bias to fix it. Push bias too far and the shadow detaches from the object — this is peter panning. The sweet spot is typically a small negative value around −0.001. Use the slider to find it live.
THREE.JS CONCEPT — Shadow Camera Frustum
The DirectionalLight's shadow is rendered through an internal OrthographicCamera. Its frustum must enclose the entire scene: too small and shadows are clipped at the edges; too large and the fixed shadow map resolution is spread over a huge area, making shadows appear blocky. Toggle Shadow cam to see the yellow frustum wireframe.
THREE.JS CONCEPT — Environment Maps & PBR
scene.environment applies an HDR cube map to every MeshStandardMaterial in the scene, providing image-based lighting (IBL). The raw HDR is pre-filtered by PMREMGenerator into a set of blurred mip-levels that match different roughness values. A rough surface samples a blurry mip; a mirror surface samples the sharpest one. This is the single biggest quality upgrade you can give a PBR scene.
