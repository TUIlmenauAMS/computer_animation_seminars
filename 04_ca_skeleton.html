Demo 2: Skeleton Viewer

Visualises the bone skeleton of a glTF character using THREE.SkeletonHelper. Switch between Fox (simple, ~24 bones) and CesiumMan (humanoid rig, ~67 bones) to see how skeleton complexity differs between models. Click any bone in the hierarchy panel to select it — a THREE.AxesHelper and pivot marker attach to the bone and update live every frame, exactly as bone selection works in Blender's Pose Mode and Maya's component editor.

Open DevTools → Console to inspect the bones array and the SkeletonHelper object. The live transform display shows world-space position and local Euler rotation for the selected bone, updating at 60 fps.

THREE.JS CONCEPT — SkeletonHelper
new THREE.SkeletonHelper(root) traverses the scene graph from root, finds every Bone object, and draws a line segment for each parent→child relationship. It is a debug overlay — not part of the character's render pipeline — and can be toggled without affecting the model. The helper auto-updates every frame as the bones move.
ANIMATION CONCEPT — AxesHelper as bone selection marker
In Blender Pose Mode and Maya's component editor, selecting a bone shows its local coordinate frame as three coloured axes: X (red), Y (green), Z (blue). This demo replicates that exactly: new THREE.AxesHelper(size) is parented directly to the selected THREE.Bone object with bone.add(axesHelper). Because Three.js bones are Object3D subclasses, the helper inherits the bone's transform automatically — no manual update needed in the render loop.
Why two models?
A skeleton is a data structure, not something unique to one character. Fox has a compact rig optimised for a quadruped — a spine chain, tail, ears, and four limb chains. CesiumMan has a standard humanoid rig with finger bones, a deeper spine, and a richer face hierarchy. Switching between them live shows that skins[].joints[] is just an array of node indices — the hierarchy panel rebuilds entirely from whatever the loader returns.
