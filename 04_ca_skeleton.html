Demo 3: Animation Playback

Drives a glTF character's skeleton with THREE.AnimationMixer — the runtime that evaluates keyframe tracks and writes the results into bone transforms every frame. Switch between models and clips, scrub the timeline, and adjust playback speed. Select any bone in the hierarchy to watch its world position and local rotation change live as the mixer drives it, exactly as you would see in Blender's Graph Editor.

Open DevTools → Console to inspect mixer, gltf.animations, and the AnimationAction object. The mixer.update(delta) call in the render loop is the single line that makes everything move.

THREE.JS CONCEPT — AnimationMixer
mixer = new THREE.AnimationMixer(characterRoot) owns all the animation state for one character. Calling mixer.update(delta) every frame advances time and writes the resulting transforms directly into the scene graph — bone positions, rotations, and scales are updated in-place, which is why the SkeletonHelper and the live transform panel reflect the motion without any additional code.
THREE.JS CONCEPT — AnimationAction & AnimationClip
An AnimationClip is the data: an array of KeyframeTracks, each targeting one bone property (e.g. Spine.quaternion). An AnimationAction is the playback controller: mixer.clipAction(clip) returns one, and calling action.play() activates it. Multiple actions can be blended simultaneously — the foundation of locomotion state machines.
ANIMATION CONCEPT — Live bone transforms during playback
Select any bone while a clip is playing and watch the rotation values in the panel change every frame. This is exactly what Blender's Graph Editor visualises as F-curves: the interpolated value of a keyframe track at the current time. The mixer evaluates the curves, writes the result into bone.quaternion, and Three.js propagates it down the scene graph via updateMatrixWorld() during the render call.
Fox vs CesiumMan — animation data
Fox ships three clips (Survey, Walk, Run) with 24 bones — a compact quadruped rig showing how a spine chain and four limb chains are keyframed independently. CesiumMan ships one walk cycle with 67 bones, including finger and facial bones, demonstrating the density of a full humanoid rig. Switching models live shows that the AnimationMixer is discarded and rebuilt per character — each model owns its own mixer instance.
