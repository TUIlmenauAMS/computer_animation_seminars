THREE.JS CONCEPT — MeshBasicMaterial ignores all lights
Its fragment shader samples only material.color (and an optional texture map) — there is no lighting calculation at all. Set ambient and directional intensity to zero: only the Basic sphere stays fully visible. This is useful for skyboxes, UI overlays, and any object that should always appear at full brightness regardless of scene lighting.
THREE.JS CONCEPT — Diffuse-only vs specular
Lambert computes dot(normal, lightDir) — intensity by angle of incidence only. Phong adds a specular term: dot(reflect(lightDir, normal), viewDir)^shininess. That's the white highlight you see on Phong but not Lambert. Orbit the point light slowly and watch: Lambert changes smoothly, Phong adds a bright flash as the light passes the reflection angle.
ANIMATION CONCEPT — Orbiting point light
The orange point light orbits the four spheres using the same pivot technique from Demo 2: pointPivot.rotation.y += orbitSpeed × delta. The light is a child of the pivot, offset on the X axis. This drives a circular orbit with zero trigonometry — the scene graph does the work. Watch how Lambert barely reacts while Phong and Standard produce a traveling specular highlight.
THREE.JS CONCEPT — PBR energy conservation
MeshStandardMaterial enforces that reflected + absorbed energy ≤ incident energy. A rough surface (roughness = 1) scatters light widely — a large, dim highlight. A smooth surface (roughness = 0) concentrates it — a tiny, bright mirror reflection. The total energy is the same. This is why PBR looks consistent under any lighting condition, unlike Phong, which can produce arbitrarily bright and energy-violating highlights.
